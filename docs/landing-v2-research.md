# Landing v2 research

Date: 20 October 2026. Public pages and docs only. No accounts were created, and no numbers below are copied onto the AIdentify page unless they are a fact about AIdentify itself.

v1 (`waitlist.html`) is a dark, black-canvas page aimed at agencies and digital / email marketers. v2 is a different audience: the owner of one small business. Agency white-label stays off that page.

## What a small-business owner is deciding

Owners of clinics, coaching practices, brokerages, studios and home-service firms are not shopping for “an agent platform”. They are trying to stop being the person every WhatsApp waits on.

What they look for, from clinic and WhatsApp guides aimed at this buyer (Medslots, NextReply, Varnan Digital, Engageo) and from how India WhatsApp tools sell:

- **Time back.** The owner is in a consult, on a site visit, or with a client. Messages pile up.
- **Not missing the lead.** Enquiries arrive at night and on Sunday. A slow reply is treated as a no.
- **24×7, on WhatsApp.** India already does this business on WhatsApp, often in Hinglish, including voice notes. A website-only bot is the wrong front door.
- **Control.** They will not let software message patients or customers on its own. Approvals, a handoff to a person, and a way to take over the chat matter more than autonomy.
- **Cost against a hire**, not against a US SDR salary. The comparison in their head is a front-desk person plus a WhatsApp tool, priced in rupees, with a GST invoice.
- **Someone to set it up.** Meta Cloud API, templates and DLT are not a Sunday-afternoon job. “We do it with you” beats “connect 1,500 apps”.
- **Data safety.** Clinics in particular: enquiries and appointments, not symptoms, prescriptions or medical advice. DPDP means a stated purpose, a real opt-in, an opt-out, and as little data as the job needs. A claim of “we are DPDP certified” would be false; “built so you can keep the purpose narrow” is the honest version.

They are already comparing **Wati, Interakt and AiSensy**: shared inbox, broadcasts, a chatbot builder, and an AI add-on. Public price bands in 2026 sit roughly from a free or ₹1,500 entry plan up to about ₹2,800–₹3,800 for a fuller WhatsApp plan, with AI often a separate add-on around ₹1,350–₹3,000 a month, plus Meta’s own per-message charges. Those products are strong at WhatsApp campaigns. They are not a team that also drafts quotes, keeps a contact timeline, and waits for approval.

## Patterns worth using

1. **Hire a team, not a tool.** Lindy, Sintra, Marblism, Artisan and HighLevel all lead with roles and a job you can picture. A feature grid alone reads as software the owner must operate.
2. **Show the work, in the hero.** Lindy’s thread (plan, checklist, “want me to pause it?”) and Decagon’s one-line concierge beat an abstract illustration. The useful part is the handoff: the agent did the prep and stopped before the irreversible step.
3. **Plain language in, a plan out.** Decagon’s procedures and Lindy’s “a sentence is all it takes” match how an owner actually wants to delegate.
4. **Industry slices.** Sierra’s industry filter is how a clinic owner finds herself. A single “for everyone” gallery makes her keep scrolling.
5. **Control next to the promise, not in a footer.** Lindy states “nothing irreversible without approval” as its own block. For clinics this has to be as prominent as the 24×7 claim.
6. **Light, quiet, a lot of space.** Decagon and Sierra are light. Most “AI agent” sites are dark purple. v1 is already dark. A warm light page with one confident accent separates v2 and feels more like a practice than a dev tool.
7. **A visible rupee price.** India WhatsApp tools publish INR. US agent tools hide behind credits or “book a demo”. An early-access teaser with “we invoice you” matches this buyer.

## Patterns to refuse

- **Invented proof.** Logo walls, star ratings, “40,000 businesses”, “$100M influenced” and testimonial carousels are the category default. AIdentify does not have that evidence. A blank “early access” line is more credible than a fake one.
- **Cute fictional staff with stock faces.** Named characters work for Sintra. They also imply a person. Use role names that match the product (Chief of Staff, Front desk, Follow-up).
- **“Autopilot” and “stop hiring humans”.** Artisan and 11x sell unattended outbound. That fights the approvals-first product, and it is the wrong tone for a clinic.
- **“1,500 integrations”.** Lindy and Sintra can say it. AIdentify’s customer channels are WhatsApp, SMS, Gmail, Google Calendar and website chat. Slack exists for the product’s own bot, not as a customer inbox promise.
- **Agency on the homepage.** GoHighLevel leads with agencies because that is their market. Here it pulls the page back to v1. One nav link is enough.
- **Crossed-out discount prices.** Sintra’s $97 → $48.50 trains distrust. Publish the rupee figure once.
- **A public marketplace.** Sintra has one. AIdentify’s playbooks are three built-in starters plus a file you export and install. Installed workflows stay drafts. Calling that a marketplace would be false.
- **Voice, one-click WhatsApp signup, DLT registration, card checkout.** None of these exist. The front desk cannot be set to full auto. A payment step never charges anyone. Saving a hostname does not buy a domain or issue a certificate.

## What v2 should make someone believe

In one screen: this is a small team I can put on WhatsApp, it will do more than book a slot, and it will not message anyone until I allow it.

Then, in order: who is on the team, what I can hand off (by job and by trade), how a plain-English goal becomes a plan I approve, the safety rules, the five channels with their real limits, a fair comparison with a hire and with a WhatsApp-only tool, the rupee teaser, and the form.

## Product facts the page may use

These are facts about the current product (v2.4), or they are labelled as targets.

- Channels a customer connects: WhatsApp (Meta Cloud API, their own credentials), SMS (Twilio or MSG91), Gmail, Google Calendar (optional), website chat. Five.
- Front desk runs when a WhatsApp or website-chat message arrives. Default is a draft in Approvals. Auto within limits can send a normal answer up to a daily cap (default 20, maximum 200). Full auto is rejected. If it is unsure, or the message tries to change the rules, it hands the chat to a person and does not send.
- A booking is stored when the confirmation is actually sent. It is written to Google Calendar only when Calendar is connected. Hours default to Monday–Friday, 10:00–18:00, Asia/Kolkata.
- Outside 24 hours of the contact’s last inbound WhatsApp, a free-form reply is refused unless the step names a template the customer already has in Meta. The whole message `STOP` (and the usual opt-out words) sets consent to opted out and nothing is sent back.
- Workflows start from a sentence. Steps that send, post or pay start on “needs approval”. The other settings are auto within limits (daily cap, allowed recipients) and auto. Account ceiling `WORKFLOW_SEND_DAILY_MAX` defaults to 50; `0` means no automatic sends. A payment step never charges anyone.
- On the hosted app the routine tick is often once a day, so a **scheduled** workflow send waits for that tick. That is not the same as the front desk, which answers on the inbound message.
- Chief of Staff plans and delegates with `ask_agent`. Weekly suggestions read only that account. Apply saves a draft or a change that still needs Confirm. It does not send. A/B tests on an email step count opens and clicks. Auto-pick copies winning words only onto a step already set to auto or auto within limits, and it never sends.
- Contact timeline: messages, notes, stages, bookings, email opens, link clicks, page visits. Opted-out contacts are not tracked.
- Playbooks: three starters (agency intake, digital campaigns, email nurture) and export / install. No contacts and no secrets in the file. Install writes only after confirm. Workflows stay drafts. This is not a public marketplace.
- Boards: groups such as Orchestrator, Desk and Factory show the work.
- Bring-your-own key: OpenRouter, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic, xAI. Stored with AES-256-GCM. Last four characters shown.
- Hosted, or self-hosted on the customer’s own server.
- Early-access prices the owner asked to publish: Starter ₹2,999 / month, Growth ₹5,999 / month, Agency ₹14,999 / month. The app does not take a card payment. Billing and Razorpay are not in this build. The usage summary is for an invoice the agency raises. It is not a tax invoice.
- Target, not a measured customer average: a first reply drafted in under 60 seconds. With the default setting the visitor does not see it until someone approves it.
- Waitlist validation (`lib/waitlist.js`): name, email, WhatsApp, city, consent, and a business type. The canonical list has **14** values, not 15: Agency, Clinic / healthcare, Coaching / education, Consulting, E-commerce / D2C, Home services, Manufacturing, Real estate, Restaurant / food, Retail shop, Salon / wellness, Software / IT, Travel, Other. Nothing is preselected. The field is required. The server accepts the string; it does not silently default it. v1’s page still ships a shorter hard-coded list; v2 uses the canonical 14.

## Design decisions for v2

- **Light first, dark optional.** Warm paper (`#f3efe6`) and ink (`#1c1915`), with a deep green accent (`#0c5c4a`) for actions. v1 stays black with a neon-green pulse. The green here is a stamp colour, not a glow.
- **Type.** Geist and Inter for UI, Fraunces for headlines. The serif is the Sierra lesson: a practice, not a dev tool. Body text stays a grotesque so forms and prices stay crisp.
- **Hero.** Copy on the left, a pure HTML/CSS “product preview” on the right: a goal in plain words, then a feed of Chief of Staff, Front desk, Follow-up and the timeline. It is labelled a preview, not a live customer account. Motion is a slow highlight and respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **No fake social proof.** A strip of product facts only, each one qualified (target, default, or a count of channels).
- **White Label** is a nav item and its own page. The main page does not explain resale, client workspaces or the agency price.
- **Same waitlist.** `POST /api/waitlist`, the same fields, honeypot and consent. Business types are the 14 from `lib/waitlist.js`, empty first option, `required`. The white-label form may preselect Agency.
- **Home is v2 on the landing site.** `AIDENTIFY_SITE=landing` copies `landing-v2.html` to `index.html`. `/v2` still serves that page. v1 stays at `/v1` as `waitlist.html`. `/white-label` is the agency page. The product server (no `AIDENTIFY_SITE=landing`) still serves the app at `/`.
